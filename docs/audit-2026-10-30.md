# Audit of the handbook, 2026-10-30

What the handbook is for, what it does well, where it fails, and a plan to make it work. Written by
a session on this repository at the owner's request ("inspect it, audit it, suggest how to improve
it significantly and a strategy how to"). It changes no rule; every change it proposes is a question
in `docs/open-questions.md` (5 to 10). Measured on version 2026-10-30 of `HANDBOOK.md` (32,340
characters) and version 2026-10-27 of `PHASES.md` (16,639 characters).

## What it is for

- One public rulebook for every Claude Code session anyone at Kowboy starts, on any repository.
  The owner runs product work through chat; agents do all the engineering; four memory files per
  repository carry decisions from one session to the next.
- It reaches a session through one line in each person's personal preferences on claude.ai: the
  session downloads the file, reads it and follows it as it would a `CLAUDE.md`.
- It grows by correction: the second time the owner corrects the same thing, the correction becomes
  a rule with its date and the owner's words.

## What it does well

- The division of labour is clear and stated once: the agent leads, the owner decides.
- The reply protocol is designed around the reader's effort, and the register gives every question a
  number for good, so the owner can answer by number in any conversation.
- The "Stop and ask" gates, the live-site rule and the one-way-decision rule are sharp and testable.
- Cold pickup (2026-10-30) is the right principle for a one-person company with many agents.
- The change history is good: every save says what changed and why, with the owner's words.
- It has been condensed once already (2026-10-27, from 49,886 to 29,847 characters), so the team
  knows the problem and the method.

## Findings

Each finding has a letter and number so a question can point to it.

### A. Does it reach the session and stay there?

- **A1. It enters at the weakest level and is the first thing forgotten.** The platform's own
  instructions and the personal preferences are system-level; a `CLAUDE.md` is loaded with them;
  the handbook, downloaded and read with a tool, is ordinary conversation content. When a long
  session is compacted (the platform summarises older parts of the conversation to make room), the
  handbook text is among the first things reduced to a summary, while the platform's reply defaults
  stay whole. The precedence the file claims in its first lines is a request to the model, not a
  mechanism. This explains "almost every session feels abandoned mid work" better than any rule
  does: late in a session the rules are gone.
- **A2. The competing reply shapes were analysed and left in place.** Register question 1 (27
  October) named three competing formats: the platform's short replies, the preferences' "Output
  shape" (answer on line one, minimal bullets) and the handbook's blocks. Questions 3 and 4 were
  withdrawn in favour of an assertion in the file (A1). The preferences still carry the competing
  "Output shape", so every reply is written under two contradictory instructions.
- **A3. The handshake is weak.** The first line reports the version and size, which proves the
  download, not the reading, and says nothing about `PHASES.md`. The version is a date, so the three
  versions saved on 28 October are indistinguishable from inside a session.
- **A4. It costs about 12,000 tokens before any work and grows about 800 characters a day.** The
  two files are 48,979 characters together (an estimate of roughly 4 characters per token). Since
  the condense of 27 October the main file has grown from 29,847 to 32,340 characters in three days.
  At that rate it passes 50,000 characters again in about three weeks, and the condense has to be
  done again by December, by hand, with the same risk of losing a rule.

### B. Can an agent follow it?

- **B1. 124 bold-led rules is too many to hold at once.** 78 in `HANDBOOK.md`, 46 in `PHASES.md`.
  A model follows a short list of sharp rules far better than a long list of good ones, and the
  rules that slip most (questions in prose, no number, going quiet, no state line) are reply-shape
  rules buried among rules about code, deletion and product texts that matter only while building.
- **B2. History is mixed into the rules.** 47 dated attributions and about 700 characters of
  parentheses in the main file, plus quoted sentences. They are valuable as history, but each makes
  the rule it sits in longer to parse and ties it to one anecdote. The handbook itself prescribes a
  `docs/decisions.md` for exactly this (date, decision, who decided, reference).
- **B3. Its own terms are not explained.** "Decide" is used as a noun about fifteen times and never
  defined; "Default" is defined only by example; "register", "staging", "converged state",
  "protected path", "gate", "golden master" and "expand, migrate, contract" are used before or
  without a definition. The handbook demands that every term be explained the first time it is used.
- **B4. Two of the four blocks have no example.** Questions and Done have one; Next and a
  discussion-mode reply have none, and Next is the block the owner said was missing most.
- **B5. Always-on and when-needed rules are not separated.** "Principles and code", "Checks",
  "Definition of done" and "Every text in the product" (about 9,000 characters) matter only while
  building or reviewing and could sit in `PHASES.md` under Build and Review. What must hold in
  every reply (who decides, the reply protocol, stop and ask, memory, session, units of work) is
  about 18,000 characters.

### C. Is it consistent with itself?

- **C1. Four rules can apply to one situation and no winner is named.** "A clear winner is done,
  not offered" (07 and 23 October), "anything new that a person sees is asked before it is built"
  (28 October), "a question gets an answer, not a change" (29 September) and "one question round,
  then execution" (21 October). An agent facing a clearly best, visible change during execution has
  to guess which rule wins. One precedence list settles it.
- **C2. "Tooling is the agent's call" against "a library added, dropped or swapped is a gate".**
  "Which library version" is tooling; adding the library is a gate. The line between them is not
  drawn.
- **C3. The numbering rule cannot be met where sessions run.** The rule of 28 October (a number is
  the question's only once it is saved to the converged copy) reversed the rule of 27 October within
  a day, and assumes every session can save to that copy mid-task. Sessions on claude.ai are held to
  one branch of their own and told never to save elsewhere (this session included), and a session
  on a client's repository has no access to this repository at all. The handbook already has the
  clause that resolves a duplicate when work is combined; the rule could rely on it.
- **C4. "Status" is defined twice**, in "Session" and in `PHASES.md`, with a small difference (the
  Done block needs what is in staging and not live, which no memory file holds).
- **C5. Rules repeated across the two files**: two failed fixes, look two items ahead, one
  component, market-leading (five times), protected path (six times), "memory current" in "Session"
  and in every phase's "Done when" line. Each repeat is a place for the two copies to drift apart.
- **C6. The repository does not keep what it prescribes.** Every repository worked in for more than
  a session must keep four memory files; this one keeps only the register, and the README says the
  save history stands in for `docs/decisions.md`. A `docs/decisions.md` here is also the natural
  home for the history in B2.
- **C7. A first correction is lost between sessions.** "Twice is a rule" counts corrections, but a
  session on a client's repository cannot write to this register, and nothing else records a first
  correction. So most rules were written on one anecdote, and the clause that deletes a rule nobody
  would break has never fired in 31 saves.

### D. How is it maintained?

- **D1. No automatic check guards the file.** Nothing verifies that the file ends with its end
  line, stays under a size, has a new and unique version when it changes, or that a condense lost no
  rule (the 27 October check was done by hand by a fresh agent). Each proposed change is combined
  into the main copy within seconds of being opened, so the proposal is a formality, and every
  session fetches the main copy directly: one bad save reaches every session at once.
- **D2. The file only grows.** With no counter for corrections (C7) and no size budget, the
  reflex is to add. 31 saves in 33 days, all additive except one condense.
- **D3. No change log a session or the owner can read.** The save history carries it well, but a
  session cannot see what changed since the version it last worked under.
- **D4. The version is a date** (A3): two changes in one day get the same version.

### E. What is missing?

- **E1. Connected services and client data.** Sessions now carry many connected services (mail,
  files, chat, shops, accounting). Nothing says what an agent may read or send through them, or
  that a client's data never leaves the client's own systems. The only privacy rules are about logs
  and secrets.
- **E2. A budget for a session.** Beyond "two failed fixes" and "the search fits the choice" there
  is no limit on time, helper agents or cost before a session stops and reports.
- **E3. Language.** Which language product texts and documents are written in (and which spelling
  the handbook uses) is unstated. It belongs in each repository's `AGENTS.md`; the handbook should
  say so.
- **E4. The owner's word against the handbook.** "The owner decides" implies the owner's
  instruction in chat wins over a handbook rule; it is not said, and the conflict rule covers only
  decisions, strategy and acceptance criteria.

## The strategy

The aim, in the owner's words of 27 October: "it contains instructions and i want you to understand
and follow them. Make so it works". Working means: read whole, held for the whole session, short
enough to follow, and proven by a feedback loop rather than by hope. Six steps, in order; each is
one item in `docs/next-steps.md` of this repository, sized in sessions.

1. **Restructure without changing a rule** (one session; the owner reads the result before it is
   saved). Question 5.
   - A core `HANDBOOK.md` of at most 18,000 characters: who decides, working with the owner, the
     reply protocol with an example of every block and of a discussion reply, the memory files,
     several repositories, stop and ask, session, units of work, twice is a rule, a glossary and a
     precedence list (C1, C2, E4).
   - The build-time rules (B5) move under Build and Review in `PHASES.md`, which stays under
     20,000 characters.
   - The history (B2) moves to a new `docs/decisions.md` here: one line per rule, with the date,
     the owner's words and the rule it created. The rule text stays; the parentheses go.
   - C3, C4 and C5 are fixed in the same change. A fresh agent checks, rule by rule, that nothing
     was lost, and its checklist is part of the proposed change.
2. **Fix the delivery** (one step for the owner, one session for the agent). Question 6.
   - The personal preferences stop describing a reply shape of their own and point to the
     handbook; the agent writes the replacement text and the owner pastes it (A2).
   - The handbook is delivered at the level of a `CLAUDE.md` wherever the platform allows it, so it
     survives compaction (A1). Which mechanisms the platform documents, and what each one needs,
     is in the section "Delivery mechanisms" below.
3. **Guard the repository** (one session). Question 7.
   - Automatic checks on every proposed change: the end line is present, each file is under its
     budget, the version changed and is unique, the formatting is clean, and no e-mail address or
     web address other than this repository's slipped in.
   - The version becomes a date with a letter (2026-10-30a), and a `CHANGELOG.md` is kept so a
     session and the owner can read what changed since a version (D3, D4).
   - A proposed-change checklist: which correction or question asked for it, and the "no rule lost"
     check for a condense.
4. **Give "twice is a rule" a memory and a budget** (a rule, then one session a month). Question 8.
   - A first correction is written where the session can write: a dated one-line "Corrections"
     list in that repository's `AGENTS.md`, named as the handbook's own text already allows.
   - One handbook session a month reads those lists (the owner names the repositories or pastes
     the lists), promotes what appears twice, deletes rules nobody broke, and checks the budget:
     when the core is over 18,000 characters, something leaves before something enters.
5. **Prove it** (one session after each handbook change, optional). Ten scripted prompts with the
   reply shape each must produce (a working reply, a discussion reply, a question, a Default, a
   blocked session, "status"), run in a fresh session by a second agent, pass or fail recorded in
   the proposed change. It turns "the reply protocol slips" from a feeling into a count.
6. **Close the gaps** (one session). Questions 9 and 10 now; E2, E3 and E4 are folded into step 1
   as one line each.

Expected result: the always-on text halves, one reply shape instead of three, rules reach the
model at a level that survives a long session, and every month the file gets shorter where nobody
needed it and longer only where the owner corrected twice.

Risks: a rule lost in the move (met by the fresh-agent check and the checklist); the owner's
preference edit is a manual step and must be done on each account; the platform may not offer a
higher delivery level for sessions started on claude.ai (see below).
